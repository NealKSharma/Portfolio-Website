It was always going to be computer science.

A future where I am not a programmer sounds terrifying and genuinely miserable. Computers have been the one constant and the most enjoyable part of my life. From playing Road Rash on a bulky old monitor with a ball mouse, to accidentally breaking my grandfather’s PC while trying to upgrade its RAM, to realizing that the computer could not support a dedicated graphics card (I was heartbroken). Every experience pulled me further into the world of computers.

When the time came to choose a career path, it was never really a decision. That choice had already been made years earlier in sixth grade with a simple YouTube search: “How to create mods for Minecraft.” I have been a gamer my entire life, and that one search introduced me to programming. If you have played Minecraft, you know it is a game built on endless possibilities and complete creative freedom. That freedom is exactly what I love about computers and programming. The ability to create, control, and build whatever you imagine.

That curiosity led me to teach myself Java long before programming was introduced academically. I have always enjoyed figuring things out on my own, experimenting, failing, learning, and building things that feel truly mine. Programming never felt like work. It felt like discovery.

Another passion of mine is space.

I grew up watching science documentaries on National Geographic, especially shows like How the Universe Works and Cosmos. I still have a notebook I started in 2017 with over a hundred pages filled with astronomy notes. From black holes to planetary rovers and spacecrafts like Pathfinder and the Voyagers, and even ideas about humanity’s future as a multi planetary species. Space keeps me curious, grounded, and excited about what lies ahead. It constantly reminds me how much there is still left to explore.

So this is where I see myself in the future. Working in a field that connects computer science with space science. Creating technology that helps us understand the universe while doing what I have always loved most.
